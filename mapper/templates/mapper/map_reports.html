Find your location. Your location will continue to be tracked on the map until you click the button again. This is useful if you are moving around and want the map to follow you. Switch between map and satellite view. Only use satellite view to compare the map with the real world. Do not use satellite view to find the precise location of dropped kerbs, because it doesn't quite line up with the Ordnance Survey map - there is a slight offset between the two. Activates 'add new report' mode. The cursor will turn into a blue cross which means you can then click on the location of the dropped kerb on the map. If you click on the wrong location, you can click on the map again to change the location. A form will appear at the bottom of the map where you can fill in the details of the dropped kerb. Show Google Street View below the map. Useful for finding the precise location of dropped kerbs. A blue arrow will appear on the map, indicating the direction you are looking at on Street View. Show location search bar to search for a specific place or location. Click in the search bar and type in a location, then click the arrow to submit your search. A list of results will appear below the search bar. Click on a result to jump to that location on the map.

Click on a road on the map above to get the streetview. If you click on a road that doesn't have streetview, it will either jump to the nearest road with streetview, or the current streetview will remain unchanged.
